# Voice and copy

**Positioning:** ameei is a **healthy snack** brand. Small-batch roasted mixes and nuts you can eat every day: real nuts, real grains, roasted instead of fried, nothing artificial. The Indian family recipes are where the flavor comes from, not the headline.

**Tagline:** *Crunch with a little bit of spice.*

ameei talks like a friend who actually reads the back of the bag: warm, dry-witted, specific, and a little cheeky. Short sentences. Concrete details over adjectives. We sound good for you without ever sounding like a lecture.

## Principles

1. **Be specific.** "Roasted slow until the sugars turn" beats "deliciously crunchy". "Peanuts, cashews, cornflakes, chili" beats "wholesome goodness".
2. **Healthy means honest, not preachy.** We earn "healthy" with facts: roasted, never fried; no artificial colors or preservatives; real nuts and grains; a portion you can see. Never shame the reader about what they ate yesterday.
3. **Roasted, never fried.** That's the core product truth and the reason we can call ourselves a healthy snack. Repeat it.
4. **Flavor is the proof.** Healthy snacks have a reputation for being boring. We're the opposite: the heat ladder, the slow burn, "the one you'll finish first". One joke per ad, max.
5. **Heritage as flavor, not as theme.** Say "from Indian family recipes" when it explains the taste. Skip "exotic", "authentic taste of India", spice markets and mystic imagery.
6. **Everyday, everywhere.** The 8 oz pouch lives in a pocket, a bag, a desk drawer, a glovebox. Snacking is a small daily habit, so we talk about the day, not the occasion.

## Claims (compliance)

"Healthy" is a regulated nutrient-content claim in the US (FDA). We use it as our **category and positioning** ("ameei healthy snacks"), and we back it with specific, true statements. We do not attach it to medical or weight outcomes.

You can claim:
- Roasted, never fried
- 100% natural, with no artificial colors, flavors or preservatives
- Made with real nuts and whole grains (name them)
- Small-batch, blended or packed the week it ships
- "Good source of protein" / "High in protein" only where the nutrition panel supports it (The Nut Case today)
- "Nut-free recipe" (Featherweight only), always with the facility caveat in fine print
- Portion facts that are on the label ("about 150 calories a handful")

Never claim:
- Weight loss, "burns fat", "boosts metabolism", or any medical or disease benefit
- "Low fat", "low calorie", "sugar-free" or "low sodium" unless the panel meets the FDA definition
- A nut-free facility, or allergen-free
- Organic, non-GMO, vegan, gluten-free or keto, unless confirmed for that product
- Comparisons that run down a named competitor

Before an ad goes out, the support line must contain at least one of the "can claim" facts. "Healthy" alone is never the support line.

## Ad copy formula

```
EYEBROW (label caps, 2–4 words)   HEALTHY SNACKS, SMALL BATCH
HEADLINE (≤7 words, italic payoff) Crunch with a little bit *of spice.*
SUPPORT (≤18 words)                Roasted, never fried. Real nuts and grains, nothing artificial, shipped fresh across the US.
CTA (2–3 words)                    Shop the Range →
```

## Reels (the ad pipeline)

- **Hook (first 2 s, on-screen text):** lead with the healthy-snack tension or the pocket moment, not the heritage. "Healthy snack, zero boredom." "The 3 pm snack that isn't a candy bar." "Fits in a blazer pocket."
- **Selling point (middle):** one claim from the list above, shown not said: the pour, the texture, the pouch zip.
- **End card:** logo, tagline, one CTA. Lowercase ameei.
- Spell-check every overlay before saving; "colors" vs "colors": use **US spelling** in anything facing US customers (colors, flavors).
